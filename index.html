Quick answers

How high should my dog's food bowl be?

No veterinary guideline sets a height. A common rule of thumb from pet-product guides puts the rim between your dog's elbow height and about 2 to 3 inches (5 to 7.5 cm) below it. A floor bowl is fine for most healthy dogs, so a raised bowl is a comfort choice.

Are raised bowls safe for large dogs?

In a study of more than 1,600 large and giant breed dogs, raised bowls were linked to about 20% of bloat (GDV) cases in large breeds and 52% in giant breeds [1]. A later evidence review found no study showing raised bowls lower that risk [2]. If your dog is a large or giant breed, ask your vet first.

How tall should a cat's bowl be?

Most healthy cats eat fine from the floor. For an older or stiff cat, a small lift with the rim a little below the elbow can make meals easier. No study has measured this, so treat it as a comfort tweak.

How wide should a cat's bowl be? Is whisker fatigue real?

In a study of 38 cats, whisker-friendly dishes made no difference to time spent eating, food dropped or food eaten, though 63% of owners said their cat preferred the wider dish [3]. A wide, shallow dish at least as wide as your cat's whisker span is a cheap thing to try.

Do slow feeder bowls work?

In a small study of five dogs, a slow feeder lowered eating speed [4]. Eating fast was one of the bloat risk factors in the large-breed study [1]. No trial has shown that slow feeders prevent bloat.

How big should the bowl be?

Big enough for one meal with room to spare. We use 1.5 times the meal volume for a regular bowl and 2 times for a slow feeder, because the ridges take up space. 1 US cup is 236.6 ml. This is our rule of thumb.

How much should I feed my dog or cat?

Start with the chart on the food bag, or work it out: resting energy is 70 × (weight in kg)0.75 kcal a day, times a life-stage factor, for example 1.6 for a spayed or neutered adult dog, 1.2 for a spayed or neutered adult cat, and 2 to 3 for puppies and kittens [5]. Divide by the calories per cup on the bag, then pick a bowl that holds one meal with room to spare. These are estimates and pets can differ by as much as 50%, so check body condition and adjust [5]. For weight loss, ask your vet for a plan.

What should I feed?

A food whose label says it is complete and balanced for your pet's life stage: growth, adult maintenance or all life stages. For a puppy that will be a large dog, the label should say the food includes growth of large size dogs [6]. That rule exists because large-breed puppies need tighter calcium limits [7].

Short legs, long ears or a flat face?

Short-legged dogs: measure the elbow height, because size charts based on weight don't account for leg length. Long-eared dogs often dip their ears in the bowl, so a narrower opening or a tilted bowl helps. Flat-faced pets usually find wide, shallow or tilted bowls easier. These are practical tips, not study results.
